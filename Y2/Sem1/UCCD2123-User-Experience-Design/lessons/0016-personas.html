Personas: one type of person, built from research

A persona is a concrete profile of one type of person the system is designed for, built from research data and PACT analysis. It helps the team design for that person's goals instead of its own. It is not one real user, and it is not an average user.

Lesson 8a introduced the People list of a PACT analysis and said a persona is built from it. This lesson says what a persona is. The book's working definition is a concrete representation of the different types of people a system is designed for (Benyon ch3, 3.3, PDF p84).

Why this matters. Every design decision needs an answer to "for whom?". A persona gives the team one clear answer to keep in mind.

Where a persona comes from

A persona starts from research, not imagination. The lecture says personas are born from user research conducted in the understanding phase (Lecture 2, PDF p14). The chosen people are then described with the PACT elements: people, activities, contexts and technologies (Benyon ch3, chapter introduction, PDF p77).

The diagram follows the Sandy persona from the Health and Fitness Companion example, in the order a team builds one. Step through it.

Stage 1. The team gathers what real people do, want and struggle with. Personas are not invented from the team's own habits.

Stage 2. The data is sorted with PACT. The People list becomes the source of the persona.

Stage 3. Sandy is one persona. Sandy needs encouragement and motivation to exercise (Lecture 2, PDF p16). Mari, the second persona, already has a fitness routine.

Stage 4. The persona is used to check a design. Because Sandy needs motivation, the team considers a reward for finishing exercise (Lecture 2, PDF p16). Mari, who enjoys exercise, would get personalised plans instead (Lecture 2, PDF p19).

Two ways a persona helps

The first way is that a persona stops the team designing for itself. The book's reason is that designers create personas so they can envisage the people they design for, and put themselves in other people's shoes (Benyon ch3, 3.3, PDF p84). The second way is that a persona makes different needs visible at once. The book says the Sandy persona would need more encouragement than the Mari persona to exercise (PDF p85).

Which statement about a persona is correct?

Right. A persona represents one type of person, and it comes from research data, not from one real user or from an average.
Not quite. A persona is not a real user, not an average, and not the designer. It is a profile of one type of person, built from research data.
