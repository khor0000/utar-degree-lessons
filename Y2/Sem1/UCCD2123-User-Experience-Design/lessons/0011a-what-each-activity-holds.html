Understanding holds PACT and requirements

Understanding produces a PACT analysis, the needs that people report, and the requirements drawn from those needs. Its job is to find out what the system must do before design decides how.

Lesson 11 named the four activities. This lesson opens the first one. It builds on the loop: understanding is the activity that starts a round, when the team finds out what people need.

Looks like Lesson 9's "requirements", but the word has a different source. Lesson 9 used it for what an activity asks of a technology. Here it is what understanding produces, from research with people.

Understanding starts with PACT

The book says understanding is about what the system must do, what it must be like, and how it fits with other things (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p78). It starts with PACT: a designer needs a clear and thorough understanding of people, activities, contexts and technologies before creative design can begin (Benyon ch7, Aims, PDF p175). Lesson 8a showed how to do a PACT analysis.

PACT is not only a picture of today. The book says a PACT analysis helps with understanding the current situation, finding possible improvements, and envisioning future situations (Benyon ch2, 2.6, PDF p72).

Needs become requirements

Need: what people want, or what they lack, in what they do now or would like to do. The book does not define it in one sentence; it writes of needs and desires (Benyon ch3, 3.1, PDF p77). Benyon ch3, 3.1; ch7, PDF p176

A requirement is the next step. Designers turn the wants and desires into requirements, which are things the product must do or qualities it must have (Benyon ch7, PDF p176).

The need comes first. Lesson 9 had a similar arrow, where a changed activity created new requirements. The difference is the cause: there the world changed, and here the designer finds the needs by studying people.

One session, many needs, and no end point

One understanding session does not find one need, and one need does not set off one session. Understanding finds several needs at once, and it keeps going after design starts. The book says a thorough understanding of requirements is rarely possible until some design work has been done and evaluated (Benyon ch7, Aims, PDF p175). Which methods to use is Lesson 13's job.

Which statement about what Understanding holds is correct?

Right. Understanding includes PACT, the needs and the requirements. PACT is also used later, to envision the future.
Not quite. Understanding holds PACT analysis, needs and requirements. Conceptual design belongs to design, and PACT is not only a snapshot of now.
