Where personas are used

Personas are used in more than one UX activity: in understanding, envisioning, design and evaluation. In expert evaluation, an expert can take on a persona, but the expert is still not a real user.

Lesson 16 defined a persona. This lesson shows where one fits in the four activities, which answers the question of how a persona can be used when no real user is in the room.

Looks like a character in a role-play exercise. The student playing the character is not the character, but the exercise tests the plan against that character's needs.

Four activities, one persona

The book says scenarios and their associated personas are a core technique for UX design, useful for understanding, envisioning, evaluation, and both conceptual and physical design (Benyon ch3, 3.4, PDF p89). The diagram follows one persona through the activities. Step through it.

Stage 1, understanding. Real people are studied, and PACT lists the variety of people, activities, contexts and technologies. This is where the data about people comes from.

Stage 2, the persona. The team summarises that research into a few personas. Each persona is a fictional profile of one type of person, grounded in the data.

Stage 3, design. Scenarios use the persona as the character doing the activity. The team asks whether each design idea serves that persona's goals.

Stage 4, evaluation. In expert-based evaluation, the expert may adopt one of the personas (Benyon ch10, 10.3, PDF p275). The expert is not a real user.

A team asks an expert to check a design by walking through a task as one persona. Which statement is correct?

Right. A persona gives the expert a viewpoint. The expert is still an expert, so the method stays expert-based.
Not quite. The expert adopts the persona as a viewpoint, but the method stays expert-based, and the expert is not a real user.
