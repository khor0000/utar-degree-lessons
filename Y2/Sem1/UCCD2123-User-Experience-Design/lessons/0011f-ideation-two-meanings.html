Ideation: two meanings in two sources

In the book, ideation means generating new ideas, and it sits inside design. The lecture slide uses the same word to mean evaluating and refining ideas. This course follows the book.

Lesson 11 named four activities, and ideation is not one of them. Lesson 11e left the word open. This lesson settles it, because a learner who meets both meanings will otherwise lose marks on one of them.

Looks like brainstorming at a stall, which is idea generation. It differs because ideation is the book's name for the creative step, and the same word is used for the checking step on the slide.

Where ideation sits in the book

The book uses ideation for the creative generation of ideas, which happens inside design (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p79). It also names the creative leap that turns stories into requirements (Benyon ch7, PDF p176), and sketching ideas during envisionment (Benyon ch8, PDF p212). So ideation is not a fifth activity. It runs through design and touches the work on either side.
