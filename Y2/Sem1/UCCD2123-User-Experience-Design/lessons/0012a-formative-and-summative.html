Formative and summative: the stage decides the label

Formative evaluation is done while the design is still changing, so its results help to shape it. Summative evaluation is done on a finished product, to judge it against criteria or a standard.

Lesson 12 showed evaluation at four stages. This lesson names the two words that describe those stages. The book uses both terms (Benyon ch10, 10.1, PDF p271).

Looks like school: weekly tests change how you study, and the final exam judges what you can do at the end. The analogy is mine, not the book's. The weekly test shapes the next week; the final exam judges the finished work.

The two definitions

Formative evaluation: evaluation done while the design is still changing, so its results shape the next version. Benyon ch10, 10.1, PDF p271

Summative evaluation: evaluation done when a product is complete, to show it meets performance criteria or a standard such as ISO 9241. Benyon ch10, 10.1, PDF p271

FormativeSummative
The one-second ruleThe result changes the design while it is still changing.The result judges a finished product against criteria.
Why both matterProblems found early are the ones the designer can still fix cheaply.It gives evidence that the product meets a customer's standard, such as required task times.
When to use whichMock-ups and prototypes.The complete product, against ISO 9241 or in-house guidelines.

The stage decides the label, not the method

A method does not make a test formative or summative. The stage does. Heuristic evaluation, for example, is formative: the book says it should not be used as a summative assessment (Benyon ch10, 10.3, PDF p277).

No single term covers both

The book names the two terms side by side and gives no single word for the pair (PDF p271). The lecture lists four stages, not the pair (Lecture 9, PDF p4). If you need a working label for your own notes, "evaluation timing" will do, but it is not a term from the book.
