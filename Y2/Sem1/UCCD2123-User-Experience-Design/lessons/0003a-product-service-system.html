Product, service, system, and UX as a verb

UX design is the activity, a verb. UX is the experience, a noun. The things UX design shapes are products, services and interactive systems. The finished thing is not the "polished" product: it is what the design work produced, and the experience is what the person lives through with it.

Lesson 3 used "design" as a verb and "UX" as a noun. This lesson fixes the nouns you will meet in the book.

Design means deciding

Design means deciding how something should work for people, not only how it looks. In an e-wallet, one design decision in each category:

  • Interaction: tap once to pay, or tap and then confirm.
  • Information: show the amount in large text before the PIN step.
  • Visual: show success with a tick and text, not colour alone.
  • Service: decide what happens if payment succeeds but the owner sees nothing.
  • Physical: place the QR sticker where a wheelchair user can scan it too.

Each bullet is a choice someone made. That is the verb at work: UX design is the whole set of such choices across the journey. The book says "UX design covers a wide range of activities" (Benyon ch1, 1.2).

Three nouns, compared

TermWhat it isExample
ProductA thing with hardware or software that you can hold or openA phone, a Touch 'n Go app
ServiceIntangible: a sequence of interactions that adds up to one achievement, over time (Benyon ch4)Maybank banking, a Grab ride
Interactive systemThe umbrella term: components, devices, products, services and software that respond to people and process content they can perceive (Benyon ch1, 1.2)A parcel-tracking system

The book uses the three words case by case. Use interactive system when you want to cover all of them at once.

Why "system" is the big word

In everyday talk, a system is several parts working together for one purpose. Benyon's parcel-tracking example is a system made of a web service, a smartphone app, delivery vans, parcel codes and code readers (ch1, 1.2). No single part is the whole thing. That is why the book calls the whole set an interactive system, and why UX design looks at all the parts together.
