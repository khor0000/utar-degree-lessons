Conceptual vs physical design

Conceptual design works out what a system must do and what people must know, in the abstract. Physical design works out how that is realised, through sequences of interaction, the allocation of functions to people and devices, and the look and feel.

Design, one of the four activities from Lesson 11, has two parts. Conceptual design asks what the system is for. Physical design asks how it will work and look (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p79). Lesson 11b used the same split to separate design from envisionment.

Why this matters. Lesson 11b showed that a sketch can be both a physical decision and envisionment. You need to tell the two design kinds apart to know which decision you are making.

Focus on. The question each kind of design answers, using the four stages below.

Skip for now. Object-action analysis, the book's method for building a conceptual model. It is a how-to, not the difference Tutorial 2 Q4 asks about.

Looks like "mental model", which appears in the same textbook passage, but it differs in whose picture it is. The conceptual design is the designer's abstract plan. The mental model is the picture a user forms in their head.

Conceptual is not the same as rough, and physical is not the same as detailed. A conceptual design can be exact. What makes it conceptual is that it stays abstract: it focuses on what the system does, not how, and it does not fix how functions and information are spread across people and devices (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p81). The book says there is no clear-cut line, only degrees of conceptuality (same page).

One example, four stages

Take a student paying for a canteen meal on the phone. Step through the stages. The first two are conceptual, and the last two are physical.

Conceptual: the things involved. A student, an order, a payment and a receipt. Nothing yet says which screen shows them.

Conceptual: the relations. An order needs a payment, and a payment gives a receipt. This says what the system must know, and it does not yet fix the layout.

Physical: the sequence. The student taps through four steps. The phone shows the QR code, and the counter scanner, a device, confirms it. Functions are shared between people and devices.

Physical: the look. Physical design also fixes colours, shapes and sizes (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p82). The Pay button is large, cyan and full width, at the bottom of the screen.

Two quick checks

Which statement about conceptual and physical design is correct?

Right. Conceptual design answers what the system is for; physical design answers how it works and looks.
Not quite. Conceptual is not rough, and physical is not the detailed version. Conceptual design is the purpose and structure; physical design is how that is realised.

Which of these decisions belongs to conceptual design?

Right. Deciding what the step must achieve, and what it needs to know, is conceptual. The other three decide how the step looks or is realised, which is physical.
Not quite. The purpose of paying and the information a step needs are conceptual. The colour, the screen order and the QR scanner are physical design.
