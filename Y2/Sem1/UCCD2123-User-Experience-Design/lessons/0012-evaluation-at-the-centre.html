Evaluation at the centre

Evaluation means checking a design idea, software, product or service against some criteria, and it happens at every stage of the UX design process, not only at the end.

Lesson 11 put evaluation at the centre of the loop. This lesson shows where evaluation happens, stage by stage. The book says everything gets evaluated at every step of the process (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p78), and the lecture says evaluation can occur at any stage, from early concepts to fully implemented systems (Lecture 9, PDF p3).

Looks like the testing step at the end of a software project, but it differs. A final test checks finished code. UX evaluation starts with a paper sketch, long before anything is built, and it repeats at every stage.

Where evaluation happens

Step through the campus food-ordering app from Lesson 11, one stage at a time. The lecture names four stages, from initial concepts to finished product (Lecture 9, PDF p4). Each stage uses its own kind of evaluation, and the bottom band shows evaluation running through all of them.

Initial concepts. A paper prototype of the order screen is compared with a competitor app (Lecture 9, PDF p4).

Design options. Two menu layouts are compared in a quick experiment. Once the app is live, A/B testing does this job, with real users (Lecture 9, PDF p4).

Usability testing. Three students try the prototype. Its results shape the next version, so this is formative evaluation (Lecture 9, PDF p4).

Finished product. The released app is checked against a standard such as ISO 9241. That is summative evaluation (Lecture 9, PDF p4). Data analytics then tracks real use.

Check the stages

Lesson 12a names the two stage words, formative and summative. Lesson 12b sorts the methods into three families. For now, the point is that the stages are not one final test.
