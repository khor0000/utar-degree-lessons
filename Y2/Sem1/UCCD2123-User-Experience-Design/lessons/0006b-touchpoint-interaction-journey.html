Touchpoint, interaction, journey

A touchpoint is a place or moment where a person meets a service; an interaction is what happens there: the person acts and the system responds. A touchpoint is one stop on the journey you met in Lesson 2.

Benyon also calls a touchpoint a service moment (ch4). A service has several touchpoints where people meet it, and its interactions happen over time, so the touchpoints should look and behave consistently with each other (ch4).

Where touchpoints sit

One product can hold several touchpoints. An e-wallet app has an onboarding screen, a payment screen, a push notification (a message the app sends to your phone) and a help chat, and each one is its own touchpoint.

Some touchpoints are not products at all. A sign, a cashier and a phone call are touchpoints too, because the person meets the service there.

So the number of touchpoints is not the number of products. Count the places where the person meets the service.

One e-wallet app has a payment screen and a help chat. How many touchpoints is that?

Right. One product, two touchpoints.
Not quite. Touchpoints are counted by the moments the person meets the service, not by products.

One journey, five touchpoints

DuitNow QR is Malaysia's national QR payment code. At a mamak stall, the customer's e-wallet app scans the code on the stall's QR sticker. Paying this way is one journey with five touchpoints. The diagram draws the line first, then zooms into the app.

Paying at a mamak stall is one journey. Each dot is a place or moment where the customer meets the service. The whole line is UX design, because all five must look and behave consistently.
