Data analytics is a family, not a field

Data analytics is one evaluation family: a method that tests a live system with logged data from real use. HCI is a field of study, the study of how people interact with technology. They are different kinds of thing, so they cannot be compared as if they were two methods.

Lesson 12b placed data analytics among the three families. Many learners hear "data" and "human-computer interaction" as two sides of one subject, so this lesson separates them.

Looks like HCI, because both use data about people. It differs: HCI is the field, and analytics is one way to evaluate a design inside it.

Field and method

The lecture defines HCI as the study of how people interact with technology (Lecture 1, PDF p16). The lecture also says the fields share common goals (Lecture 1, PDF p15). Data analytics, by contrast, is one of the three evaluation families in Lesson 12b. It is a way of checking a design, not a field of study.

Data analyticsHCI
The one-second ruleA method that tests a live system with logged data from real use.A field that studies how people interact with technology.
Why both matterIt shows what many real users do on a live system.It gives the goals and methods that evaluation, design and usability all draw on.
When to use whichA question about a live page, such as which of two versions gets more clicks.A question about the field, not about one design.

A classmate says HCI is an evaluation method, just like A/B testing. Which correction is correct?

Right. HCI is the field, and analytics is one family of methods.
Not quite. HCI is the study of how people interact with technology. Data analytics is one evaluation family used within that work.
