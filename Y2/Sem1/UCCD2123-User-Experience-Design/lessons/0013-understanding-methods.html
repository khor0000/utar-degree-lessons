Understanding: methods

Understanding finds out what people need, and what the system has to do and be like. Its key methods are interviews, questionnaires, probes, card sorting, working with groups, fieldwork and artefact collection, and good understanding combines asking people with watching them.

Lesson 11a said understanding holds the PACT analysis, the needs and the requirements. This lesson lists the methods that find them. The book calls understanding user research, because designers must research the people, activities and contexts before they can write down requirements (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p78).

Looks like a feedback form on a website, which asks people what they think. It differs in one way. Understanding uses several methods, and watching people is one of them, because what people say and what they do can differ.

Ask and watch

People find some activities hard to put into words, and an interviewee may describe the official procedure rather than what they really do (Benyon ch7, 7.8, PDF p200). So the methods pair asking with watching. In the made-up case below, a student says one thing and is seen doing another.

Ask. The student says they check the noticeboard for room changes. This is what the student says they do.

Watch. In this made-up case, following the student shows the noticeboard is skipped. They open the group chat, ask a friend, then walk to the room.

Requirement. The two methods together give a requirement: room changes in one place, with a push alert. Asking alone would have produced the wrong one.

Structured and semi-structured interviews

A structured interview follows questions written beforehand, word for word. A semi-structured interview has a checklist of topics, and the interviewer can follow up on what comes up (Benyon ch7, 7.3, PDF p180; Lecture 6, PDF p12).

Why does fieldwork belong in understanding, when interviews already exist?

Right. Asking gives one side of the story, and watching checks what people actually do.
Not quite. Interviews are kept. The reason to watch is that what people describe and what they do can differ.
