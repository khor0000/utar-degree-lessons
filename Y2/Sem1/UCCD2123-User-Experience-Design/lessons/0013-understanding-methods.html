Understanding: methods

Understanding finds out what a system has to do, what it has to be like and how it fits with other things, and its key methods are interviews, questionnaires, probes, card sorting, working with groups, fieldwork and artefact collection.

Understanding is one of the four activities from Lesson 11. The book also calls it "user research", because designers have to research the people, activities and contexts in their domain before they can write down the requirements (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p78).

Requirement: "what a product must do or the qualities it must have." Lecture 6, PDF p3

Why this matters. Every requirement in the later lessons comes from what you find out here. If the understanding is wrong, the design is wrong from the start.

Focus on. The seven methods in the table below, and why watching people is needed as well as asking them.

How you'll be tested. Tutorial 2 Q3, answered in writing, closed book.

Looks like a feedback form on a website, which asks people what they think. It differs in one way. Understanding uses several methods, and watching people is one of them, because what people say and what they do can differ.

Understanding is not only asking users what they want. People find some activities hard to put into words: "many manual procedures fall into this category". An interviewee may also "describe the 'official' procedure rather than how something is actually done in practice" (Benyon ch7, 7.8, PDF p200). So the methods pair asking with watching. In the made-up case below, a student who says one thing is seen doing another.

Ask. The student says they check the noticeboard for room changes. This is what the student says they do.

Watch. In this made-up case, following the student shows the noticeboard is skipped. They open the group chat, ask a friend, then walk to the room.

Requirement. The two methods together give a requirement: room changes in one place, with a push alert. Asking alone would have produced the wrong one.

The key methods

The methods also differ in how structured they are. A structured interview "uses questions that are developed beforehand" and follows the wording exactly. A semi-structured interview has a checklist of topics, and the interviewer can follow up on what comes up (Benyon ch7, 7.3, PDF p180 to p181; Lecture 6, PDF p14).

Produce it • closed book

Close this lesson, and answer T2 Q3 on paper: What are some key methods used in the "Understanding" phase of UX design?

reveal model answer

Key understanding methods are interviews (structured, semi-structured or unstructured conversations about needs and problems), questionnaires (broad surveys across many people), probes (self-documentation over time), card sorting (how users naturally categorize information), working with groups (focus groups and workshops), fieldwork or observation of people in their real environment, and artefact collection, which studies the documents and forms people create and use. Designers combine asking with observing, because people find some activities hard to describe and may describe the official procedure instead of what they really do. Together these methods produce the requirements, the functions the system must perform and the qualities it must have.
