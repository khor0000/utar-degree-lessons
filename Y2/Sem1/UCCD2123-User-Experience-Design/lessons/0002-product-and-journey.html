Product and journey, pinned down

The product is whatever you are responsible for designing. A journey is one person working towards one goal. A journey is a sequence of interactions over time, taken to get something done. Benyon uses the same idea for a service: "a sequence of interactions that constitutes a whole, more abstract achievement" (ch4).

The slides use both words without a tight definition, so these are working meanings to help you think. Do not quote them as definitions in an exam.

Designing a product means designing one touchpoint well. Designing a journey means making all the touchpoints fit together, so the whole stays coherent (Benyon ch4, 4.3). The book's wider view is the "whole user journey" (ch4, 4.2).

A product does not have to be physical: apps, websites and services count. A painting on a wall does not respond to people, so it is out of scope. A gallery's audio-guide app or ticket kiosk is in scope, because the book's list includes "a tourist attraction" (Benyon ch1, 1.2).

How wide is a journey? You choose the zoom

Narrow. Designing the payment screen? Zoom in on scan and pay.

Medium. Fixing lunch queues? Include choosing the stall and getting the food.

Wide. Asking why students abandon the app? Follow them for months. The width comes from the goal you are designing for.

A timeline like the one above is one way to draw a journey. The book says there "is no standard" way to represent one (Benyon ch4, 4.3). A list of touchpoints shows the same journey another way. The common pieces are before, during and after experiences, plus the touchpoints (Benyon ch4, 4.3).

How many journeys? Count people and goals

One person with one goal makes one line.

Three products sit on that line, but it is still one journey. Products do not add lines.

A different person or a different goal adds a line. So the stall owner has their own journey.

Working rule (not in the book): one goal gives one journey. Two goals give two journeys, which may share touchpoints.

While paying, the student switches from wi-fi to mobile data.
The night before, the student topped up the e-wallet.
