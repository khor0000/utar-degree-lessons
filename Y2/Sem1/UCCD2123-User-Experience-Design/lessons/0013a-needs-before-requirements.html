Needs before requirements

A user need is what people do or want to do, why, and where their current way goes wrong. A requirement is what the product must do, or a quality it must have. Designers get from one to the other with a creative leap, which the book calls ideation.

Lesson 13 gave the methods that find needs. Lesson 11a said needs come before requirements. This lesson defines both words and the step between them, so that the methods have something exact to produce.

Looks like a complaint at the canteen counter, and the fix the stall writes from it. The complaint is the need; the fix is the requirement. The stall still has to think of the fix.

User needs

The book does not give a one-sentence definition of user needs. It describes the focus of understanding as what people do or might want to do, how and why, and any problems they have (Benyon ch7, PDF p175). That is the working definition used here.

User needs: what people do or want to do, why, and where their current way of doing it goes wrong. Working definition from Benyon ch7, PDF p175 and p178 (not a book sentence)

Requirements

Requirement: something the product must do, or a quality that the product must have (Benyon ch7, PDF p176). Lecture 6 splits requirements into functional ones, which say what the system must do, and non-functional ones, which say what qualities it must have (Lecture 6, PDF p5).

The book contrasts two things. The gain is what the new design will deliver, and the pain is the problem people have with the current way of doing things (Benyon ch7, 7.2, PDF p178). Requirements describe the gain; the needs describe the pain.

The creative leap

Getting from needs to requirements takes a creative leap. The book calls it ideation, the generating of new ideas (Benyon ch7, PDF p176). Lesson 11f explains that word in more detail. The step is why the book calls the analysis, design and evaluation cycle so iterative (same page).
