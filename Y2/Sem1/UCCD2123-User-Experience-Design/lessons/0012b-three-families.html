Three families of evaluation: who checks, and when

The three families are expert-based, participant-based and data analytics. They differ in who does the checking and when. Expert methods are quick but can miss what real users find, and participant methods show the real picture but need a prototype.

Lesson 12a split the evaluation by stage. This lesson sorts the methods. The book sorts them into the same three types (Benyon ch10, 10.1, PDF p268), and the lecture uses the same three (Lecture 9, PDF p4).

Looks like three kinds of customer feedback: a mystery shopper, a focus panel, and the till data. The mystery shopper is quick and expert. The panel is real people. The till data arrives only once the shop is open.

Speed against realism

Expert-based methods pick up significant usability problems quickly, but experts sometimes miss detailed problems that real users find difficult (Benyon ch10, 10.1, PDF p268). So participant methods must be used at some point in development (same page). Heuristic evaluation is formative, not summative (Benyon ch10, 10.3, PDF p277).

A/B testing: the data family in one case

A/B testing is a data analytics method. Real users are randomly split between two live versions of a page, and the team compares which one performs better (Benyon ch10, PDF p275; Lecture 9, PDF p11). It needs a live page, so it cannot test a paper mock-up.

A team wants to know which of two versions of a live checkout page gets more clicks. Which method fits?

Right. A/B testing compares two live versions using real use data.
Not quite. The team needs real use data from a live page, and that is data analytics. The other three use an envisioned design, not live traffic.
