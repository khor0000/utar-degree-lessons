Why the rough form comes first, and the techniques

A rough form is used first because it is cheap to change, and a rough form may be thrown away. The common envisionment techniques are sketches, storyboards, mood boards, maps, wireframes and lo-fi or hi-fi prototypes.

Lesson 15 said envisionment makes ideas visible. This lesson explains why the first version should be rough, and lists the techniques the book and lecture use.

Looks like a rough draft of an essay before the final copy. The draft is for you and your group to change. The final copy is for the lecturer, and it is expensive to redo.

Rough first, because it is cheap to discard

The book's test is whether a representation suits its purpose. A sketch drawn on the back of an envelope may be fine for generating an idea and explaining it to a colleague, but not for giving to a client (Benyon ch8, 8.1, PDF p212). Sketches are quick, inexpensive and disposable, and the designer should be happy to throw them away (Benyon ch8, 8.2, PDF p213). The lecture says a lo-fi prototype should be produced quickly and discarded easily (Lecture 7, PDF p27).

A polished version has its place, but usually later and for an outside audience. No single technique will produce the perfect design, so the team uses several (Benyon ch8, 8.1, PDF p212).
