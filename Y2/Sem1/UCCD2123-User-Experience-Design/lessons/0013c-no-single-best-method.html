No single best method: each has a weakness

No understanding method is the best one. The book does not rank them, and each has a weakness. A designer combines methods so that one method makes up for another's limits, and checks one against another.

Lesson 13b used five methods on one case. This lesson answers the question a learner asks next: which method, and what does each cost?

Looks like choosing a bus, a taxi or a bike for one trip. It differs in that the methods are combined: a team often uses more than one on the same question.

Combining methods

The book describes the designer as a bricoleur, someone who improvises with whatever methods suit the setting (Benyon ch7, PDF p207). Using more than one technique helps to compensate for individual limitations (PDF p208).

Triangulation means investigating a domain from at least two complementary positions, so that the findings can be checked against each other (Benyon ch7, PDF p207). In the noticeboard case from Lesson 13, the interview gave the noticeboard, and fieldwork showed the group chat. Only the two together gave a reliable requirement.

Which statement about choosing understanding methods is correct?

Right. There is no single best method. The choice depends on the question, and combining methods makes up for each one's limits.

Close lessons 13 to 13c. Answer Q3 on paper: What are some key methods used in the "Understanding" phase of UX design? Then name one weakness of an interview and one of fieldwork.

reveal model answer

Key understanding methods are interviews (structured, semi-structured or unstructured conversations about needs and problems), questionnaires (broad surveys across many people), probes (self-documentation over time), card sorting (how users naturally categorise information), working with groups (focus groups and workshops), fieldwork (observing people in their real environment) and artefact collection (studying the documents and forms people create and use). Designers combine asking with observing, because people find some activities hard to describe and may describe the official procedure instead of what they really do. Together these methods produce the requirements. An interview can only tell the interviewer about activities the person is aware of. Fieldwork takes at least three times the raw video length to analyse.

Primary source: Benyon, chapter 7, sections 7.3 to 7.9 (PDF p180 to p205), and PDF p207 to p208 (bricolage, triangulation). Lecture 6, PDF p11.
