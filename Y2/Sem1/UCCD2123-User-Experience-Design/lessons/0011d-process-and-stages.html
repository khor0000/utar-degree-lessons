The process and its stages: no fixed start, no fixed order

The process is the whole set of activities across a project. A stage is one of the four activities, and a stage is a place you can enter, not a numbered step. The process has no fixed start and no fixed order.

Lesson 11c showed that the next step after an evaluation is not fixed. This lesson fixes the vocabulary, then sets the iterative picture against the linear one that many learners expect.

Looks like a recipe: step 1, step 2, step 3, and then it is done. It differs because the steps are not fixed. A recipe is the picture the book rejects. Here you may enter at any activity and return to any of them.

"Process" and "stage" in the slides

The lecture calls the UX process a generic process model: the practical steps a team follows to take a product from an idea to a launched, usable experience (Lecture 2, PDF p4). That is the whole sequence of activities across a project.

A stage is the slide's word for one of the four activities. The same slide says the process can start at any stage (Lecture 2, PDF p4). The book uses "point" for the same idea: the process can start at any point (Benyon ch3, 3.2, PDF p78).

Linear and iterative, side by side

The slide sets a linear process against an iterative process (Lecture 2, PDF p2). The book takes the iterative side. The phrase "done once from top to bottom" describes the linear picture, which the book rejects.
