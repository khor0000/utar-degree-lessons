Glossary

TermMeaningLesson
User experience (UX)The whole journey a person goes through using a product, including how their view of it changes.0001
UX design (UXD)Developing interactive systems, products and services that are usable and enjoyable.0001
ProductWorking meaning: whatever you are responsible for designing.0002
JourneyWorking meaning: everything one person goes through to reach one goal. Count one per person, per goal.0002
Interactive systemTechnology that responds to people's actions and handles content they can perceive. A phone app is one; a chair is not.0001
Human-centredDesigning around people's needs, goals and contexts.0003
Machine-centred viewSeeing people as vague and machines as precise, so people must adapt to the machine.0003
PACTPeople, Activities, Contexts, Technologies: the four elements to consider when designing interactive systems.0008
PACT analysisListing the variety of each PACT element in a domain to scope a design problem.0008
DomainOne area of activity the design serves. One PACT analysis covers one domain, which may hold several journeys.0008a
RequirementsWhat a system must do and the qualities it should have.0009
OpportunitiesNew possibilities a technology offers, which change activities.0009
Device ecologySeveral devices working together to create an environment.0005
HCIHuman-computer interaction: the study of how people interact with technology.0006
Interaction designThe design of interactive systems.0006
UsabilityThe ease with which users can effectively and efficiently interact with a system to achieve their goals.0007
EffectiveThe system lets the user achieve the goal.0007
EfficientThe goal is reached with minimal effort or time.0007
TouchpointA place where a person meets a service (an app screen, a counter, a rider at the gate).Benyon ch4
InteractionWhat happens at a touchpoint: the person acts and the system responds.0006b
Service"A sequence of interactions that constitutes a whole, more abstract achievement"; intangible.Benyon ch4
Feedback (principle)The system tells people what effect their action had.Benyon ch5 principle 7
Constraint (principle)Design that blocks wrong actions, e.g. scan a QR instead of typing 16 digits.Benyon ch5 principle 9
Recovery (principle)Letting people recover from mistakes quickly, e.g. a biometric login when the PIN is forgotten.Benyon ch5 principle 8
Trade-offGaining one quality by giving up another, e.g. speed vs security.Benyon ch2 2.6
UnderstandingWhat the system or service has to do, what it has to be like and how it fits with other things; also called user research.0011a
NeedWhat people want, or what they lack, in what they do now or would like to do. The book writes of "needs and desires" rather than defining it.0011a
PrototypeA working mock-up of a design idea, so that people can react to something real.0015a
IterativeThe process goes round its activities in repeated rounds, and each round refines the design. It is not linear.0011e
StageThe lecture slides' word for one of the four activities (the book says "point"). The process can start at any stage.0011d
Idea generationBrainstorming as many ideas as possible, without judging their quality yet. The lecture slide's sense.0011f
IdeationThe creative process of generating ideas for a design, in the book's sense. The lecture slide uses it to mean evaluating and refining ideas.0011f
EvaluationReviewing, trying out or testing a design idea, software, product or service to discover if it meets some criteria.0012
Expert-based evaluationEvaluation by a usability expert or UX designer who reviews an envisioned design, without real users.0012b
Participant-based evaluationEvaluation in which real people use an envisioned design. Also called user testing.0012b
Heuristic evaluationAn expert checks a design against a list of principles for good design.0012b
HeuristicsThe principles or guidelines for good design that an expert checks a design against.0012b
Cognitive walkthroughAn expert steps through a design task by task, asking at each step whether users will take the right action.0012b
Data analyticsGathering data from a deployed system to assess its performance and users' behaviour.0012c
A/B testingRandomly splitting users between two live versions of a page and comparing a measure such as click-through rate.0012b
Heat mapA visual map of the most clicked areas of a page, or of where people look, from click or eye-tracking data.0012b
Page viewsThe number of times a page is viewed; a count used in data analytics.0012b
Formative evaluationEvaluation done while the design is still changing, so its results help to shape it.0012a
Summative evaluationEvaluation of a finished product against performance criteria or a usability standard.0012a
ISO 9241A usability standard, named in the lecture as an example of criteria for a finished product.0012a
User testingAnother name for participant-based evaluation, in which people use an envisioned design.0012b
Cooperative evaluationA participant method in which participants work as co-evaluators rather than passive subjects.0012b
Controlled experimentA participant test that compares designs by changing one factor (the independent variable) and measuring its effect (the dependent variable).0012b
EnvisionmentMaking design ideas visible, such as a sketch or a prototype, so the designer and the users can react to them.0015
StoryboardThe key moments of an experience, in sequence, like a comic strip.0015a
Mood boardThe look and feel: images, colours and the emotional tone.0015b
WireframeThe layout and structure of pages, without colour or fonts.0015a
Wizard of OzA Wizard of Oz prototype has a human play the feature that does not yet exist.0015a
PersonaA concrete profile of one type of person the system is designed for, built from research data and PACT analysis. It is not one real user, and not an average user.0016
Conceptual scenarioA scenario that describes an activity without fixing the technology or the interface, so it can generate ideas.0017
Concrete scenarioA scenario with specific design decisions added, such as which device is used and how the screen works, so it can be used to envision and evaluate one particular design.0017
Use caseA case of how the system is used, formalized from a concrete scenario and given to programmers.0017
AbstractionThe level of detail in a scenario: the difference between conceptual and concrete scenarios is abstraction, not length.0017a
Conceptual designDesigning a system in the abstract: the information content and functions it needs to achieve its purpose.0014
Physical designMaking the design concrete: structuring interactions into sequences, allocating functions and knowledge between people and devices, and fixing the look and feel.0014
Representational designThe part of physical design that fixes colours, shapes and sizes.0014
Mental modelThe model a person builds of how a system works, by interacting with it.0014b
Entity-relationship modelA diagram of the things (entities) in a system and how they relate. A conceptual modelling tool.0014a
InterviewTalking to stakeholders to learn what they want and what problems they have.0013
Structured interviewAn interview that follows questions written beforehand, word for word.0013
Semi-structured interviewAn interview with a checklist of topics, where the interviewer can follow up on what comes up.0013
Unstructured interviewA general conversation that starts from one broad question and follows the interviewee's lead.0013
QuestionnaireA broad survey that quantifies behaviours and opinions across a large group.0013
ProbesCreative self-documentation tasks that gather insights over time.0013
Card sortingAn exercise that reveals how users naturally categorize information.0013
Working with groupsA method that works with groups of people. The most common example is the focus group.0013
Focus groupA facilitated discussion among users to explore diverse perspectives.0013
WorkshopA collaborative session for brainstorming and idea generation.0013
FieldworkDirect observation of people and their activities in their real environment.0013
Artefact collectionAnalysis of the tools and documents people create and use.0013
Conceptual modelThe designer's abstract description of the main objects in a system, their attributes and the relationships between them. It names no screen or device.0014a
ObjectA thing the system deals with, such as an order.0014a
AttributeA fact that describes one object, such as the pickup time of an order.0014a
RelationshipA link between two objects, stated as how many of one can link to how many of the other, using 1 or m.0014a
System imageThe interface, the behaviour of the system and any documentation, through which users build their mental model.0014b
StoryA real-world experience of people, with the small details a formal account would leave out. The first of the four scenario types.0017
Degree of concretenessHow far a scenario fixes a device, an interface or the split of functions between people and devices. Scenarios have degrees, not a clean break.0017b
Bounce rateThe share of visitors who leave a site at once, without looking at any content. A data analytics measure.0012b
Participatory heuristic evaluationA heuristic review in which participants join the experts as work-domain experts, checking the design against heuristics.0012b
Information architectureHow a site's content is organised and labelled, so that people can find things.0013c
User needsWhat people do or want to do, why, and where their current way of doing it goes wrong. A working definition: the book does not give one sentence.0013a
BricoleurA designer who improvises with whatever methods suit the setting, in the book's sense.0013c
TriangulationInvestigating a domain from at least two complementary positions, so that findings can be checked against each other.0013c
ExternalisingGetting a thought out of your head and onto something that can be looked at, changed and shared. The book's aim for envisionment.0015
Design workbookA collection of the design's own proposals and materials, kept through a project to investigate options (Gaver's term in the book).0015b
Gain and painGain is what a new design will deliver; pain is the problem people have with the current way of doing things.0013a
