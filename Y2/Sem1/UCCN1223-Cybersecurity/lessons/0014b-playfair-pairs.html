Playfair: cutting the message into pairs

Yes: before Playfair looks at the grid, the message is cut into pairs of two letters, from the left. A pair with two equal letters gets a filler X between them, and an odd length gets a pad Z after the last letter.

Bridge: lesson 14a built the key square, the grid that does the encrypting. This lesson is the step before it: the message is cut into chunks of two, and only then is each chunk looked up in the grid.

Recap: the MONARCHY square (lesson 14a)

You will not use the grid in this lesson. Keep it in mind only as the thing the pairs are later looked up in.

Looks like columnar's padding X (lesson 13a), but... both add an X to fill a gap, so the idea of filling looks the same. The reason differs. Columnar's X fills the empty cells at the end of the last row, so every column is the same length. Playfair's filler X is added only when a pair has two equal letters, so that pair has two different letters to work from. Playfair's pad Z has a third job, for an odd length.
New term: digraph. (1) What it is: a pair of two plaintext letters, cut from the message. (2) What it holds: two letters, such as IN. (3) Why it is needed: Playfair encrypts one digraph at a time, so the cut decides every output pair.
New term: filler. (1) What it is: an extra letter X, put into the message between two equal letters. (2) What it holds: the letter X. (3) Why it is needed: a pair with two equal letters has no two different cells to work from.
New term: pad. (1) What it is: an extra letter Z, put after the last letter of an odd-length message. (2) What it holds: the letter Z. (3) Why it is needed: a pair needs a partner, and one letter left over cannot form a pair.

1. Cut the message into pairs

The message has its spaces removed, then it is cut from left to right into pairs. Three cases change the cut. Each has its own walk.

1a. A repeated letter in one pair: TREE (slide 46)

If the two letters of a pair are the same, the pair has no two different cells to work from. So a filler goes between them, and the pair becomes two different letters.

T R E E I S G R E E N
[T R]
[T R] [E X]     E E becomes E X
[T R] [E X] [E I] ...   the second E starts pair 3
TR EX EI SG RE EN
General rule (repeat): if both letters of a pair are the same, write the filler X between them, make that pair, and start the next pair with the second letter. The filler is a letter added only to make the pair legal.

1b. An odd length: INSTRUMENTS (slide 44)

Eleven letters cannot all be paired. The last letter needs a partner, so a pad is added after it.

I N S T R U M E N T S    (11 letters)
IN ST RU ME NT  S
IN: I N   ST: S T   RU: R U   ME: M E   NT: N T   (no equal pairs)
IN ST RU ME NT SZ
General rule (odd length): add the pad Z after the last letter, so every pair is complete. Slides 39, 44 and 45 all use Z for this.

1c. Two equal letters in different pairs: ATTACK (slide 45)

ATTACK has two Ts, so its letters look like a repeat. They are not, because each T is in a different pair.

A T T A C K    (6 letters)
AT   TA   CK
AT: no    TA: no    CK: no    (no filler)
AT TA CK
