1. Count the rows first

The receiver knows the keyword, so they know how many columns there are. The number of letters in the ciphertext gives the number of rows: the ciphertext length divided by the keyword length.

3. The rule, and why the number order matters

General rule (decrypt): (1) number the keyword's letters alphabetically; (2) rows = ciphertext length ÷ keyword length; (3) fill the columns in number order, top to bottom, giving each column the next block of letters; (4) read the rows left to right; (5) drop the padding X at the end.
Why fill in number order?

The ciphertext is the columns joined end to end, so each block goes back in its own column.

Critical Exam Trap • Fill the Numbered Column

On decryption, the first block of letters goes into the column numbered 1, not the first column on the page.

Reveal the answer, step by step
  1. Number the keyword: P is 5, L is 3, A is 1, N is 4, E is 2, T is 6.
  2. Rows: 18 letters ÷ 6 letters per keyword = 3 rows. Draw an empty grid, 3 rows by 6 columns, with the numbers 5 3 1 4 2 6 above P L A N E T.
  3. Number 1 is the A column (column 3). Its 3 letters are E T D, top to bottom.
  4. Number 2 is the E column (column 5). Its next 3 letters are M E E.
  5. Number 3 is the L column (column 2): E T I. Number 4 is the N column (column 4): T H G. Number 5 is the P column (column 1): M A R. Number 6 is the T column (column 6): E B X.
  6. Read the rows: MEETME / ATTHEB / RIDGEX. Joined, that is MEETMEATTHEBRIDGEX.
  7. Drop the padding X: the message is MEETMEATTHEBRIDGE.
