The Caesar cipher with a two-row strip

Write the alphabet on top. Under it, write the alphabet again, starting at the letter the key lands on (key 3 lands on D). To encrypt, find the letter on top and read the one below it. To decrypt, find the letter on the bottom and read the one above it. You never count backwards.

Why this matters: Caesar is the "move every letter 3 forward" recipe from lesson 8. The strip turns that recipe into lookups, and the keyword ciphers reuse it. Focus on: building the strip and reading it both ways. Skip for now: the number method (A=0 and so on). It comes in lesson 15a, modular arithmetic. How you'll be tested: encrypt and decrypt by hand, with no calculator (old-format papers: May 26 Q2a, 4 marks; Oct 25 Q1a–b, 8 marks). Also Lab 2 Q1.

Lesson 8 gave the recipe. Here it becomes a strip you read with your eyes.

Looks like the recipe in lesson 8 and the "shift LEFT" rule on slide 21, but…
Recipe (8, slide 21)Strip (this lesson)
SameThe shift is the same: 3 places, forward to encrypt, back to decrypt
DifferentYou count the places, forward or backYou look the letter up in a table
WhyCounting backwards is the step you said you cannot do, so the strip replaces it with a lookup

1. Encrypt HELLO with key 3

Press Next, or the arrow keys:

plainABCDEFGHIJKLM
cipherDEFGHIJKLMNOP
plainNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
cipherQRSTUVWXYZABC
plainABCDEFGHIJKLM
cipherDEFGHIJKLMNOP
plainNOPQRSTUVWXYZ
cipherQRSTUVWXYZABC
topHELLO
belowKHOOR

So HELLO → KHOOR. Spaces and punctuation stay where they are, in the same place.

2. Decrypt KHOOR: bottom to top

Use the same strip. Find each letter on the bottom row and read the letter above it.

plainABCDEFGHIJKLM
cipherDEFGHIJKLMNOP
aboveHELLO
bottomKHOOR
You might reasonably wonder: can I decrypt KHOOR by encrypting it again with key 3? No. That gives NKRRU. Decrypting is the same strip read the other way, not a second encryption.

3. The end of the alphabet takes care of itself

Encrypt ZOO with key 3: Z has C under it, so ZOO → CRR.

Critical Exam Trap • Show the Strip

Old-format questions say "show your steps". Write the two-row strip at the top of your answer: that is your working. An answer with no strip can lose the method marks.

4. Check yourself

Check 1: slide 22, key 6 (fresh input)

Encrypt KNOWLEDGE IS THE KEY TO SUCCESS with key 6. Try it on paper before you open the reveal.

Reveal, step by step
  1. Key 6: the bottom row starts at G, so the bottom row is G H I… F.
  2. First word, letter by letter: K→Q, N→T, O→U, W→C, L→R, E→K, D→J, G→M, E→K. So KNOWLEDGE becomes QTUCRKJMK.
  3. Full answer: QTUCRKJMK OY ZNK QKE ZU YAIIKYY. The spaces stay put.
Check 2: slide 23, decrypt (fresh input)

Slide 23 gives IEHKX YKIAXOZE OY YCKKZ and says key 4. Decrypt only the first word, IEHKX, with key 4. Does it read as English?

Reveal, step by step
  1. Key 4: the bottom row starts at E. Pairs: I→E, E→A, H→D, K→G, X→T.
  2. Result: EADGT. That is not a word, so key 4 is not the key.
  3. Try key 6, the bottom row starts at G. Pairs: I→C, E→Y, H→B, K→E, X→R. Result: CYBER.
  4. The full line with key 6 is CYBER SECURITY IS SWEET. The slide's key 4 does not give English. Only key 6 does.

KHOOR was made with key 3. Which step gets HELLO back?

Yes. Find K on the bottom row and read above it: H. (Counting 3 places back by hand gives the same answer.)
Not this one. Reading top to bottom, encrypting again, or counting forward all move each letter 3 more places forward and give NKRRU. Decryption goes back: read bottom to top.
