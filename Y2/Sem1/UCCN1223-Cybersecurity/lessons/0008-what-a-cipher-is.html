The cryptosystem: five elements, and where the cipher fits

A cryptosystem is the whole setup for secret messages, made of five elements (slide 7). The cipher is two of them: the encryption algorithm and the decryption algorithm, a matched pair of methods. The key, the plaintext and the ciphertext are separate from it.

Why this matters: every Topic 3 question scrambles or unscrambles a message, and this lesson gives you the words those questions use. Focus on: the five elements of a cryptosystem, and why the key, not the recipe, must stay secret. Skip for now: the history of ciphers, block and stream ciphers, Shannon's list of what makes a cipher secure, and the two ways to scramble and the two kinds of key (both in the next lesson, 9). Topic 3 is about scrambling messages with keys, so that only a holder of the right key can read them. This lesson names the elements of a cryptosystem, and the next lesson sorts ciphers into kinds. The lessons after that scramble real messages by hand, one cipher at a time.

A cryptosystem is the whole setup for secret messages. What it is: the cipher's two algorithms (the encryption algorithm and the decryption algorithm) plus the plaintext and the ciphertext and the key. What it holds: the five elements on Topic 3a slide 7; slide 6 introduces the word as "a system for encryption and decryption". Why it is needed: the sender and the receiver must agree on all of these parts, or the message cannot be sent and read.

Lesson 7 showed that telnet sends password123 where any listener on the wire can read it, while ssh sends the same data scrambled. This lesson explains what that scrambling is, and where it is used.

1. The problem: your message passes through other people's hands

Ali sends Siti a message over the network: PAY RM50 TO SITI. On the way it passes through routers and Wi-Fi that Ali does not control. An intruder there can do four things (Topic 3a slide 4):

The intruder…ExampleWhat is lost
Intercepts: reads itEve reads PAY RM50 TO SITIConfidentiality: only the right reader can read it
Modifies: changes itSiti receives PAY RM500 TO EVEIntegrity: the message is no longer what Ali sent
Fabricates: makes up a new oneEve sends From Ali: PAY RM900 TO EVEAuthenticity: Ali never sent it
Blocks: stops itSiti receives nothingAvailability: it never arrives

Encryption fixes the first row. If Eve intercepts SDB UP50 WR VLWL, she has the message but cannot read it. The other three rows need tools from Topic 3b, not from this lesson.

You might reasonably wonder: is encryption just hiding the message, compressing it, or encoding it the way Base64 does? No. Hiding puts the message out of sight, but anyone who finds it can still read it. Compression makes the message shorter, and anyone with the matching tool can undo that. Base64 is a public way to write data as letters and digits, and anyone can reverse it with no secret at all. Encryption is different because only a secret key can reverse it. Slide 6 uses the word "encoding" for encryption. In this course, use "encrypt" for that job, and keep "encode" for formats like Base64.

CYBER SECURITY is the plaintext. The key is 3. The recipe is "move each letter 3 places forward".

Sent: FBEHU VHFXULWB. Eve has the ciphertext. Without the key, she cannot turn it back.

Receiver runs the Caesar recipe backwards: FBEHU VHFXULWB → CYBER SECURITY.
