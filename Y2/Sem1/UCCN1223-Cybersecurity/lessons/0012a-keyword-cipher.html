The plain keyword cipher: a two-row strip built from a word

A keyword cipher builds its cipher row from a keyword: write the keyword first, then the unused letters, and encrypt and decrypt with that strip exactly as in lesson 11.

Why this matters: the keyword ciphers are the next group of substitution ciphers on slide 28, and the Oct 2025 paper asked a keyword cipher by hand. Focus on: building the strip from a keyword, and writing a repeated keyword letter once. Skip for now: the keyword mixed cipher (lesson 12b) and the transposed and interrupted variants (slides 33 and 35, lessons 12c and 12d). How you'll be tested: encrypt and decrypt by hand, with no calculator. Oct 2025 Q1(d) asked a plain keyword cipher with keyword SECURITY, 2 marks for each word (old format, a hint only).

Bridge: lesson 11's strip was the alphabet shifted by one number. Here the bottom row starts with a word, so you write it out rather than count to it.

1. The plain keyword cipher, keyword WIRELESS

A keyword cipher is a substitution cipher whose cipher row starts with a keyword.

Anatomy of a keyword cipher. (1) What it is: a substitution cipher whose strip is built from a word. (2) What value it holds: one 26-letter cipher row, fixed for the whole message. (3) Why it is needed: it gives a strip without a shift number, so a word can be the key.

Keyword W I R E L E S S. Written once: W I R E L S (6 letters).

Unused letters, in order: A B C D F G H J K M N O P Q T U V X Y Z. That is 20 letters. 6 + 20 = 26.

STUDENTS → PQTELJQP. Eight letters in, eight out. The slide prints PQTEJQP, which has seven. E becomes L, so the L must be there. The slide has a typo.

You might reasonably wonder: is this transposition? No. Substitution: each plain letter is replaced.
Critical Exam Trap • Write Each Keyword Letter Once

A repeated keyword letter is written once in the cipher row. Writing it twice puts a duplicate in the strip.

Monoalphabetic cipher is the slide 27 section name for these ciphers: (1) what it is: a cipher that uses one fixed cipher row for the whole message. (2) what value it holds: one plain-to-cipher letter pair per letter. (3) why needed: it names the family, so you know one strip is enough to encrypt and decrypt.
