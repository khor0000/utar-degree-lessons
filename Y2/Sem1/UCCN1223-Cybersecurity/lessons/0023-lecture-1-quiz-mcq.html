Lecture 1 quiz check

Lecture 1 is five terms and one idea: protect the system, know the threat, weigh the risk. These questions are multiple choice, like the quiz.

Looks like the terms in Lecture 1, such as attack and threat, which people often swap.

The terms in one line each

TermOne-line meaning (Lecture 1, slides 18 to 19)
VulnerabilityA weakness that a threat can exploit
ThreatAnything that can exploit a vulnerability and cause harm
RiskThe chance of that happening (likelihood) combined with the damage it would cause (impact)
AttackAn intentional attempt to gain unauthorised access or disrupt
FirewallA device or software that monitors and controls network traffic

Check yourself

A weakness in a system that a threat can exploit is called a:

Yes. The weakness is the vulnerability. The threat is what uses it.
The weakness itself is the vulnerability. A threat is the thing that uses it.

The likelihood of a threat exploiting a vulnerability, and the impact it may cause, is called:

Yes. Risk is likelihood combined with impact.
This one combines a chance and an impact, which is risk. The others name a single item.

Lecture 1 names three levels of protection. Which set is correct?

Yes. Slide 7: personal, organizational, government.
Lecture 1 slide 7 lists personal, organizational and government.

Which standard does Lecture 1 cite for industry security?

Yes. Slide 14 cites ISO/IEC 27001, an international information-security standard, and NIST, a US standards body.
Slide 14 names ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST. ISO 9001 is about quality, not security.

An intentional attempt to gain unauthorised access to a system is an:

Yes. An attack is the intentional act.
The intentional act is the attack. The others are a weakness, a chance, or a defence.
