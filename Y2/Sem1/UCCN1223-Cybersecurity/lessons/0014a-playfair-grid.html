Playfair: the 5 × 5 key square

Yes: Playfair's key is a 5 × 5 grid. Write the keyword into it, fill the other cells with the unused letters, and I and J share one cell.

Why this matters: the grid is the key. Where two letters sit in it decides what they turn into. Lesson 11 changed one letter at a time, so this grid is the first thing in Playfair that is new. Focus on: building the grid from a keyword. Skip for now: cutting the message into pairs (lesson 14b), and the encryption rules (lesson 14c). The deck does not teach decryption. How you'll be tested: by hand, no calculator. Old-format papers gave Playfair 7 marks (May 2026 Q2) and 4 marks (Oct 2025 Q1). Whether the new format keeps it: not confirmed.
Bridge: the two-row strip in lesson 11 used one line of letters and one shift. Playfair uses a grid of rows and columns instead of a line.
Looks like the keyword strip (lesson 12a), but... both start from a keyword and put its letters first. The keyword strip still changes one letter at a time, along one line. Playfair fills a 5 × 5 grid and works on two letters together. Why it differs: the grid lets the two letters of a pair affect each other's output, which one line of letters cannot do.
Looks like columnar transposition (lesson 13a), but... both use a keyword and a grid. Columnar fills its grid with the message and reads the columns in keyword order, so no letter changes. Playfair fills its grid from the keyword first, and every message letter is replaced, not moved.
New term: key square. (1) What it is: a 5 × 5 grid of letters. (2) What it holds: 25 letters, one per cell, with I and J sharing one cell. (3) Why it is needed: it is the key. Where two letters sit in the grid decides their output.

General rule (grid): (1) write the keyword, skipping any letter already written; (2) add the unused letters from A to Z in order, leaving out J; (3) the 25 cells hold 25 letters, and the I cell also stands for J.

Yes. I and J share one cell, so the letter J is changed to I before the grid is used.
J has no cell of its own. The I cell stands for both I and J, so a J in the message becomes I.

Build the grid for keyword NETWORK. Which letter is in row 2, column 1?

Yes. Row 2 is R K A B C, so column 1 is R.
Write the keyword first: N E T W O R K, with no repeats. Then fill the unused letters in alphabetical order, skipping J. Row 2 starts with the keyword letters after O, which are R and K.
